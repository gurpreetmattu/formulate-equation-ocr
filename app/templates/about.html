About Formulate

A deep learning pipeline that converts images of printed math equations into LaTeX and MathML, so they can be reused in papers, documents, and web pages.

Reading a printed equation is trivial for a person; turning it back into usable markup by hand is not. Formulate exists to close that gap — point it at a scanned or photographed formula and get back LaTeX and MathML you can drop straight into a paper, a document, or a web page, instead of retyping it from scratch.

How it works

Image
Preprocess
LWDSC-SA Encoder
BiLSTM + Attention Decoder
LaTeX + MathML
  • Preprocess — the image is converted to grayscale, cropped to its content bounding box, sharpness/contrast-enhanced, and Otsu-binarized to pure black and white, then resized and normalized so the model sees a consistent input regardless of the original photo's size, framing, or lighting.
  • LWDSC-SA Encoder — short for Lightweight Depthwise-Separable-Conv + Spatial-Attention. A compact convolutional network (using depthwise-separable convolutions to keep the parameter count small) scans the image, with a spatial attention layer that weights the regions that matter most, and a row-wise BiLSTM turns the resulting 2D feature map into a left-to-right sequence.
  • BiLSTM + Attention Decoder — a second BiLSTM refines that sequence, then an attention-based decoder generates the output one symbol at a time, attending back to the relevant part of the sequence for each token it writes.
  • LaTeX + MathML — the predicted token sequence is assembled into LaTeX, and MathML is derived from that LaTeX, so both formats are ready to use.

Beam search decoding is shown as the primary result since it explores more candidate sequences before choosing one; greedy decoding runs alongside it as a faster, secondary comparison. In practice the two are usually close, and either can come out ahead on a given equation — when they differ, the highlighted tokens make it easy to compare and pick the one that looks right.

Key features

  • End-to-end OCR — image in, editable LaTeX and MathML out.
  • Dual decoding — greedy and beam search results shown together for comparison.
  • Visual verification — the preprocessed input and rendered LaTeX are shown alongside the raw output.
  • Crop & rotate before submitting — adjust a skewed photo or trim the frame right in the browser, no external editor needed.
  • Local result history — your last few uploads stay one click away, kept only in your browser.
  • Correctness feedback — a quick thumbs up/down on each result, so you can flag whether it looked right.

Tips & limitations

Get better results

  • Crop tightly around the equation before submitting — use the built-in Adjust tool if the source photo is skewed or has extra margin.
  • Flat, even lighting and a straight-on angle beat a shadowed or angled phone photo.
  • Works best on a single equation per image, rather than a full page of mixed text and math.

Known limitations

  • Best on high-quality scans of printed (not handwritten) equations.
  • May require post-editing for deeply nested or unusual formulas.

Dataset: im2latex-100k. Inspired by Mathpix, im2latex, and the PyTorch/Flask communities.

Privacy

Uploaded images are processed in memory to produce a prediction and are never written to disk or stored on the server. Result history and feedback votes live only in your browser's local storage and are never sent anywhere — history can be cleared any time with the "Clear" button on the Recognize page, or by clearing this site's data in your browser.
